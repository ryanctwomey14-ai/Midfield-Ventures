You are a limited partner. We are the general partner.

Each property is owned by its own LLC. Midfield sponsors and operates it; you own a share of it.

Your liability is limited to what you invest. You never sign on the loan, and lenders have no recourse to you personally.

Midfield — general partner

  • Finds and underwrites the property
  • Negotiates the purchase and the debt, and signs on the loan
  • Funds and manages the renovation
  • Hires and oversees property management
  • Handles insurance, taxes, compliance and audits
  • Reports to partners and files the partnership return
  • Decides when to refinance or sell

You — limited partner

  • Review the offering and ask whatever you want
  • Sign the subscription documents electronically
  • Verify accreditation, once
  • Wire your investment to the escrow account named in the documents
  • Receive distributions
  • Read a quarterly report
  • Give your K-1 to your CPA

Appreciation

Value we create, not wait for

An apartment building is worth its income divided by a market cap rate. Raising income raises the value on purpose — we do not need the market to cooperate.

K-1

Depreciation against your income

The building depreciates on paper while it holds its value in reality. That paper loss passes through to you and often exceeds the cash you received.

Monthly

Someone else pays down the loan

Residents’ rent retires the mortgage month after month. Equity accumulates quietly whether or not values move, and it is yours at sale.

Why the K-1 is often the best part.

Real estate is depreciated on paper even while it appreciates in reality. That paper loss flows through to you.

Each year you receive a Schedule K-1 reporting your share of income, loss and deductions. In the early years of a value-add deal, accelerated depreciation from a cost segregation study often produces a reported loss even in a year you received cash distributions.

What that does to your return depends entirely on your situation — your income, whether you or your spouse qualify as a real estate professional, and your passive activity carryforwards. We are not tax advisors and we will not pretend otherwise. Take the K-1 to your CPA.

When your K-1 arrives

We target April. If one will be late, we tell you before your CPA does, and we send what you need to file an extension.

Where you will owe

Partnerships file in the state the property sits in, so you may have a non-resident filing in that state as well as your own.

If you invest through an IRA

Debt-financed income inside a retirement account can trigger UBIT. It is often still worth doing — but check with your CPA before, not after.

The ones that come up on every call.

Can I get my money out early?

No. There is no redemption window and no secondary market. In genuine hardship we will try to help you find another partner to buy your interest, but nothing is promised and the operating agreement governs. Invest only capital you will not need for 3–7 years.

What happens if the property underperforms?

Distributions get reduced or paused first — that is the shock absorber, and it is why the preferred return accrues rather than disappears. In a severe case the partnership could require additional capital or lose the property to the lender, in which case you can lose your entire investment. Every offering states these scenarios explicitly.

Do I have to be accredited?

For most offerings, yes. Broadly: $200,000 of individual income ($300,000 jointly) in each of the last two years, or a net worth over $1 million excluding your primary residence. Certain professional licences also qualify. We verify before you can subscribe.

Can I invest through an entity or a trust?

Yes — LLCs, trusts, self-directed IRAs and solo 401(k)s are all common. Tell us the entity when you subscribe so the documents are drawn correctly the first time.

How much do most people invest?

The minimum is typically $25,000. Most first-time partners start near the minimum and increase on their second or third deal, which is exactly how we would suggest doing it.
