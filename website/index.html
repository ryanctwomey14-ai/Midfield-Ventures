We buy underperforming assets in markets the price hasn’t caught up to.

Every Midfield deal follows the same three moves: find an emerging market with real job and population growth, buy a community running below what it should, and capture the difference. No development risk, no ground-up construction, no exotic structures.

Acquire

We target 80–150-unit workforce communities in emerging markets — too large for local landlords, too small for institutional funds. That gap is where the pricing is still sane.

Improve

Renovated interiors, corrected expenses, professional management. Rent rises because the property is genuinely better — not because we bet on the market.

Distribute

Cash flow is distributed quarterly. Depreciation passes through on your K-1. At sale or refinance, partners receive their capital plus their share of the gain.

Why we buy Class B and C apartments

Apartments are graded by age, condition and rent level. Class A is new construction at the top of the market. Class B and C are older buildings, usually well located, renting below what new construction charges.

We buy Class B and C for four reasons:

  • Rents are lower, so more people can afford them. Occupancy stays steady.
  • When the economy slows, renters move down from Class A into B and C. Demand rises rather than falls.
  • Almost nobody builds new Class B and C, because construction costs only work at Class A rents. Supply stays flat while demand grows.
  • The buildings are old enough to improve. Renovating units raises rent, and higher rent raises the value of the property.

Class A does not offer that. It is already finished, it competes directly with every new building nearby, and it loses residents first when money gets tight.

The questions people ask before they invest.

If yours isn’t here, ask it on the call. We would rather lose a deal than have a partner who didn’t understand what they signed.

What is the minimum investment?

Typically $25,000 per offering, though it varies by deal. Minimums are stated in each offering’s documents.

How long is my money committed?

Plan on 3–7 years. There is no redemption window and no secondary market. This should be capital you will not need for anything else — if that is not true, do not invest.

Do I have to be an accredited investor?

For most of our offerings, yes. Generally that means $200,000 of individual income (or $300,000 jointly) in each of the last two years, or a net worth over $1 million excluding your primary residence. Certain professional licenses also qualify. We verify accreditation before you can subscribe.

How did your properties hold up when rates spiked?

[Answer this one honestly and specifically. Address bridge debt versus fixed-rate agency debt, whether any distributions were paused, and whether any capital calls were made. Investors have been asking every sponsor this since 2023 and a vague answer is read as a bad answer.]

How do you get paid?

An acquisition fee of [X]%, an asset management fee of [X]%, and a share of profits only after partners receive a [X]% preferred return and their capital back. Every fee is disclosed in the offering documents — and we would rather walk you through them than have you find them in an appendix.

Can I invest through my IRA or a trust?

Yes. Self-directed IRAs, solo 401(k)s, trusts and LLCs are all common. Retirement accounts can trigger UBIT on debt-financed income — talk to your CPA first, and we can point you to custodians other partners use.

When do I get my K-1?

We target [MONTH]. If a K-1 will be late, we tell you before your CPA does, and we cover what you need to file an extension.
