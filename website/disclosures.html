Risk of loss

Investing in private real estate involves substantial risk, including the risk of losing your entire investment. Risks include but are not limited to:

  • Illiquidity. There is no public market for these interests, no redemption right, and you should be prepared to hold for the entire term or longer.
  • Leverage. Properties are acquired with debt, which magnifies both gains and losses. Lenders are repaid before investors.
  • Interest rate and valuation risk. Rising rates increase borrowing costs and can reduce property values on unchanged income.
  • Execution risk. Renovation budgets may overrun, timelines may extend, and business plans may not be achieved.
  • Market and supply risk. Local economic conditions, new construction, insurance costs and regulatory changes may adversely affect performance.
  • Additional capital. A partnership may require additional capital contributions, and declining to participate may dilute your interest.
  • Sponsor and key-person risk. Performance depends significantly on the sponsor’s judgement and continued involvement.
  • Tax risk. Tax treatment depends on your circumstances and may change; anticipated benefits may not be realised.

This list is not exhaustive. Complete risk factors are set out in each offering’s documents and should be read in full before investing.
