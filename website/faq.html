The basics

Start here if this is your first private real estate investment.

What exactly am I buying?

A share of a limited liability company that owns one specific apartment property. You are a limited partner; we are the general partner and operate the asset. You own real estate through a partnership — not a fund share, not a note, and not a share of our company.

What is the minimum investment?

Typically $25,000 per offering, though it varies by deal and is always stated in that offering's documents. Most first-time partners start at or near the minimum and increase on a later deal, which is exactly how we would suggest doing it.

Do I have to be an accredited investor?

For most of our offerings, yes. Broadly, that means $200,000 of individual income ($300,000 jointly) in each of the last two years with a reasonable expectation of the same this year, or a net worth over $1 million excluding your primary residence. Certain professional licences — Series 7, 65 or 82 — also qualify. We verify before you can subscribe.

What if I am not accredited?

Join the education list anyway. You will receive the primer and our market notes, and we will let you know if an offering opens that you can participate in. Plenty of our partners spent a year on that list first.

How is this different from buying a REIT?

A public REIT is liquid, trades with the stock market, and pays dividends taxed as ordinary income with no depreciation passed to you. A partnership interest is illiquid and uncorrelated to public equities, and depreciation flows through to you on a K-1. Neither is better in the abstract — they solve different problems, and we will say so if a REIT is the better fit for you.

How is this different from buying a rental myself?

Scale, financing and your calendar. A hundred-unit property diversifies vacancy across a hundred leases rather than one, qualifies for institutional debt you cannot access personally, and carries professional management. The trade is control: you do not choose the tenants, the contractor or the sale date.

Can I invest through an LLC, a trust, or with a partner?

Yes to all three. Tell us the investing entity before you subscribe so the documents are drawn correctly the first time.

Money and returns

What you can expect to receive, and what we take.

What returns do you target?

Targets are stated per offering, because they depend entirely on the property, the price and the debt. Representative targets are around [X]% preferred return, [X–X]% cash-on-cash once stabilised, and a [X.X–X.X]x equity multiple over the hold.

Those are targets built on assumptions that may not hold. They are not projections of your result and they are not guaranteed. You can lose money.

When do distributions start, and how often?

Typically quarterly, beginning after the property stabilises — often [6–12] months after closing. Early cash goes into the renovation that creates the value, which is the point of a value-add deal.

What is a preferred return, in plain English?

It is an annual return you receive on your capital before we take any share of profits. If a period's cash flow does not cover it, the shortfall normally accrues and must be paid before we participate. It is a priority in the payment order — not a guarantee that the money will be there.

How do you get paid?

An acquisition fee of [X]%, an asset management fee of [X]% of collected revenue, a construction management fee on renovation work, a disposition fee at sale, and a share of profits only after partners receive their preferred return and 100% of their capital back.

Every fee is listed in full on the how investing works page and in each offering's documents. If a sponsor will not show you this on a first call, that tells you something.

How long is my money committed?

Plan on 3–7 years. There is no redemption window and no secondary market. If there is a realistic chance you will need this money sooner, do not invest it here — and we will say the same thing on the call.

Can I get out early if I really need to?

Not on demand. In genuine hardship we will try to help you find another partner to purchase your interest, but nothing is promised and the operating agreement governs any transfer.

What is a capital call, and could I face one?

A capital call is a request for additional money when a property needs more than it has. It is possible, it is disclosed as a risk in every offering, and we underwrite with reserves specifically to make it unlikely. Any sponsor who tells you it can never happen is telling you something they cannot know.

Can I lose my entire investment?

Yes. Private real estate is bought with leverage, and the lender is paid before you are. A severe enough decline in value or income can wipe out the equity entirely. That is the honest floor of the risk, and it is why this should be one position among several rather than the whole plan.

What kind of debt do you use?

[State your policy: fixed-rate agency debt where possible, and the conditions under which you would use bridge debt, including required rate caps and reserve levels. This answer is doing more work than any return figure on the site.]

What if the local market gets overbuilt?

New construction almost exclusively targets the top of the market. Our thesis is that middle-market communities receive residents trading down from new Class A product rather than losing them to it. That said, a submarket can absorb thousands of units and stay soft for two years, and we underwrite supply pipelines before we buy.

What happens if a distribution has to be paused?

You hear it from us before the quarterly report is due, with the reason and what we are doing about it. Unpaid preferred return normally accrues and is paid before we take any profit share.

How do I know this is not a scam?

Fair question, and the right one to ask. Verify us: our names and backgrounds are on the about page, our properties are real addresses you can drive to, our offering documents are prepared by securities counsel, and funds are wired to an escrow account — never to us personally. Ask for investor references and call them. Any sponsor who resists that request has given you your answer.

Taxes

We are not tax advisors, and these answers are general. Your CPA has the final word on all of it.

What is a K-1 and when will I get it?

A K-1 reports your share of the partnership's income, losses and deductions. We target [MONTH]. If one will be late, we tell you before your CPA does and send what you need to file an extension.

How does depreciation actually help me?

Buildings are depreciated on paper even while they hold or gain value. A cost segregation study accelerates that deduction into the early years. The result is that your reported taxable income from the investment is often far below the cash you received — sometimes a reported loss in a year you were paid.

Whether that loss can offset your other income depends on the passive activity rules and your specific situation. Do not assume it will.

Will I owe tax in another state?

Possibly. The partnership files where the property is, so you may have a non-resident return in that state as well as your own. Your CPA will handle it; budget for a slightly more expensive return.

What is depreciation recapture?

When the property sells, the depreciation you benefited from is partly taxed back. It is a real cost and it is accounted for in the after-tax picture — sponsors who quote returns without mentioning it are quoting an incomplete number.

Can I use a 1031 exchange?

Exchanging into a partnership interest generally does not qualify, which surprises people. [If you offer tenant-in-common or 721 UPREIT structures, describe them here. If not, say so plainly.] Speak with your qualified intermediary before your exchange window opens, not after.

What about investing through my IRA?

Common and workable through a self-directed custodian. The catch is UBIT: debt-financed income inside a retirement account can be taxable. It is often still worth doing — check with your CPA first.

Process and reporting

What happens, in what order, and what lands in your inbox.

What happens after I request the packet?

You receive the investor primer, our acquisition criteria and a sample quarterly report. If you asked for a call, Tim or Kim reaches out within a business day. If you did not, nobody calls you.

How are allocations decided when a deal opens?

Generally first come, first served among investors on the list. Offerings can fill quickly, which is the practical reason to join the list before a property you like appears rather than after.

How do I actually send the money?

By wire, to the escrow account named in your subscription documents. We will never send you new or updated wire instructions by email. If you receive any, call us at the number on this site before sending anything. Wire fraud is the most common way investors in private real estate lose money and it has nothing to do with the property.

What reporting will I receive?

A quarterly report covering occupancy, rent movement, renovation progress and financials against budget; quarterly distributions by ACH; an annual K-1; portal access to documents and statements; and a direct call whenever something material changes.

Can I visit the property?

Yes. It is a real building with a real address and we encourage it. Tell us when you are going and we will let the manager know.

How do you decide when to sell?

When the numbers say to, not on a fixed calendar. If selling into a bad market would harm partners, holding longer is usually the better decision, and the operating agreement allows for it. We would rather extend a hold than lock in a poor exit to meet a projected date.
